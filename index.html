Artemis II Crew Shatters Distance Record on Historic Moon Flyby

Four astronauts traveled 252,756 miles from Earth—farther than any humans since Apollo 17 in 1972.

Four astronauts aboard NASA's Orion spacecraft made history on April 6 when they completed a breathtaking lunar flyby, reaching 252,756 miles from Earth—a new record for the farthest humans have ventured from our planet since Apollo 17 in 1972. The crew of Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Canadian Space Agency astronaut Jeremy Hansen captured more than 7,000 photographs of the Moon's far side during their seven-hour flyby.

Adding to the wonder, the crew witnessed a rare solar eclipse from their extraordinary vantage point, with Commander Wiseman describing the view as "beyond words." All four astronauts splashed down safely. The success of Artemis II paves the way for Artemis III, which aims to land the first woman and first person of color on the lunar surface—a milestone that now feels closer than ever.

682 People in Dino Suits Stomp to a Guinness World Record

The University of Calgary celebrated its 60th birthday in the most prehistoric way possible.

On April 11, the University of Calgary gathered 682 students, professors, staff, and children in inflatable dinosaur costumes to claim a Guinness World Record for the largest-ever gathering of dino-costumed humans. The event, dubbed "Rex's Jurassic Jamboree," crushed the previous record of 468.

Historic Milestone: Wind and Sun Beat Fossil Fuels Across All of Europe

For the first time ever, renewables generated more EU electricity than coal, oil, and gas combined.

Wind and solar generated 30% of EU electricity in 2025, edging past fossil fuels at 29%—a milestone that would have seemed impossible a decade ago, per analysis from clean-energy think tank Ember. Solar alone grew by 20%+ for the fourth year running. Coal fell to an all-time low of 9.2%.

Fourteen of the EU's 27 member states now produce more power from clean sources than fossils. Meanwhile Norway hit its own milestone: 97% of new car sales are now electric vehicles, with EVs outnumbering diesel cars on the road. "This is a turning point that can't be undone," one researcher declared.

US Roads Are Safer Than They've Been in Over a Century

Traffic deaths fell 6.7% in 2025, reaching the second-lowest fatality rate in 100-plus years of records.

Traffic deaths in the US dropped to 36,640 in 2025—a 6.7% decline—giving the nation its second-lowest traffic fatality rate in more than a century of records (1.10 deaths per 100M vehicle miles). Fatalities fell in 39 states, DC, and Puerto Rico.

The fourth quarter of 2025 marked the 15th consecutive quarter of declining road deaths—a remarkable 3-plus year run. Experts credit advances in vehicle tech (automatic emergency braking, lane-departure warnings) and greater awareness of impaired driving. Every 1% reduction in the fatality rate represents hundreds of lives saved.

Giant Pandas Graduate From 'Endangered' to 'Vulnerable'

Fifty years of conservation effort in China officially lifted the beloved bear off the world's most-at-risk species list.

The IUCN upgraded the giant panda from "Endangered" to "Vulnerable," recognizing a wild population now at approximately 1,900—a 17% increase over the previous decade. China established 67 panda reserves protecting nearly two-thirds of all wild giant pandas.

"This is what's possible when communities commit to conservation," said WWF's panda director. Crucially, bamboo corridor restoration reconnected fragmented habitats. "We can genuinely bring species back from the brink," said one researcher. "The panda is living proof."

Smart Glasses Are Quietly Giving Blind People a New Freedom

Ray-Ban Meta AI glasses let visually impaired users read menus, navigate streets, and identify objects—entirely hands-free.

Ray-Ban Meta AI smart glasses pair a camera with AI to narrate the world in real time for people with visual impairments. Wearers can glance at a menu and hear it read aloud, look toward a sign and receive spoken directions, or scan a grocery shelf to identify products—all without touching a phone.

The American Foundation for the Blind called them "a genuine leap" in assistive tech. A Be My Eyes partnership connects wearers with sighted volunteers via a live camera feed. At $299—far cheaper than previous dedicated assistive devices—this technology is changing lives right now.

Science Confirms: Optimism Is a Brain Health Superpower

A US-led study found that people who score higher on optimism measures have a 15% lower risk of developing dementia.

A major US-led study found that people with higher optimism scores have a 15% lower risk of developing dementia. Each six-point increase on the researchers' optimism scale was linked to a meaningful reduction in risk, even after accounting for education, exercise, and social engagement.

Researchers believe optimistic people manage stress better and build greater "cognitive reserve." Best of all: optimism can be practiced. Writing down three things you're grateful for each day has been shown to measurably shift a person's outlook. So the prescription for brain health may simply be expecting tomorrow to be a little brighter.

Missing 180 Years, Giant Tortoises Finally Come Home to Galápagos

158 tortoises bred from century-old DNA were released on Floreana Island in a landmark conservation triumph.

On February 20, Galápagos island Floreana welcomed back its giant tortoises for the first time in ~180 years. The Charles Darwin Foundation released 158 juveniles carrying the long-extinct lineage's DNA—bred from individuals on neighboring Isabela Island identified by scientists in 2008 as having Floreana ancestry.

The $15M project first cleared invasive rats, cats, and pigs from Floreana before the tortoises arrived. As ecosystem engineers, tortoises reshape vegetation through grazing and seed dispersal, benefiting dozens of other species. "We've proven," said one researcher, "that even 180-year-old losses are not always permanent."

Arizona Neighbors Log 500 Acts of Kindness in Just One Week

The Fiesta Sports Foundation's sixth annual Kindness Arizona Week wrapped with a state-wide wave of generosity earning a Governor's proclamation.

Across Arizona in late March, hundreds of volunteers completed eight service projects and documented nearly 500 individual acts of kindness during Kindness Arizona Week—earning an official proclamation from Governor Katie Hobbs.
