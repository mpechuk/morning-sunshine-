Artemis II Crew Shatters Record for Farthest Humans From Earth

Four astronauts flew 252,757 miles from home, further than any human has ever traveled, circling the far side of the Moon.

On April 6, 2026, the crew of NASA's Artemis II mission made history by traveling 252,757 miles (406,773 km) from Earth as they looped around the far side of the Moon — the greatest distance any human has ever been from our home planet.

The milestone eclipsed the Apollo 13 record set in 1970 and marks a triumphant step toward returning humans to the lunar surface. Mission commander Reid Wiseman called the view of Earth from that distance "the most humbling and breathtaking thing I have ever witnessed."

The four-person crew splashed down safely to jubilant celebrations at NASA's Johnson Space Center, paving the way for Artemis III, which aims to land the first woman and first person of color on the Moon.

Giant Panda No Longer Endangered After Decades of Conservation

China's decades-long programme of habitat protection and captive breeding has paid off: the giant panda is officially 'vulnerable,' not endangered.

In a triumph for wildlife conservation, the giant panda has been downgraded from 'Endangered' to 'Vulnerable' on the IUCN Red List. Wild panda populations have grown steadily over the past 20 years, with an estimated 1,900 individuals now roaming China's mountain forests.

The turnaround is credited to China's sweeping bamboo forest restoration projects, the expansion of over 60 dedicated nature reserves, and a successful captive breeding programme that has returned animals to the wild.

"This is proof that conservation works when governments, scientists, and communities commit to the long game," said WWF's species director. Researchers caution that climate change still threatens bamboo habitats, but today's news is being celebrated as one of the greatest wildlife recoveries in modern history.

More Than 10% of the World's Ocean Is Now Officially Protected

Nations reached a landmark milestone for ocean conservation, with protections now covering an area larger than North America.

For the first time in history, more than 10 percent of the world's ocean is under formal protection — a milestone environmentalists have worked toward for decades. The achievement was confirmed this month as several new marine protected areas came into force under the High Seas Treaty.

The protected zones ban or strictly limit damaging activities such as deep-sea mining, overfishing, and industrial pollution, giving marine ecosystems space to recover. Scientists say healthy oceans are critical to regulating the climate, producing oxygen, and supporting the livelihoods of more than 3 billion people.

The UN's ocean chief called it "a historic turning point for our blue planet," adding that the goal is now to protect 30% of the ocean by 2030 — an ambition that suddenly feels within reach.

AI Maps Gut Bacteria to Detect 90% of Colon Cancers — No Colonoscopy Needed

A simple stool sample analysed by artificial intelligence could soon replace uncomfortable screenings for colorectal cancer.

Scientists have developed an AI model that analyses the microbiome in a routine stool sample and correctly identifies 90 percent of colorectal cancer cases, according to a major new study. The breakthrough could dramatically change how one of the world's most common cancers is detected.

Currently, colonoscopy — an invasive procedure that requires sedation and hospital time — is the gold-standard screening tool. The new AI test is non-invasive, inexpensive, and can be done at home, potentially removing barriers that stop millions of people from getting screened.

The research team is now seeking regulatory approval for clinical use. Experts say wider, easier screening could catch cancers much earlier, when they are most treatable, potentially saving hundreds of thousands of lives per year worldwide.

Solar Panels Shatter Efficiency Record With 34% Power Conversion

Next-generation tandem perovskite solar cells convert over a third of sunlight into electricity, blowing past silicon panels.

Researchers have achieved a power conversion efficiency of over 34 percent with tandem perovskite solar cells — a remarkable jump compared with the roughly 24 percent achieved by the best commercial silicon panels available today. The result, published this month, sets a new world record and could accelerate the transition to affordable, clean electricity.

Perovskite cells are cheaper and easier to manufacture than silicon, and the tandem design layers two materials that each capture different parts of the solar spectrum, squeezing far more energy from every ray of sunlight.

Industry analysts say this efficiency milestone could halve the cost of solar electricity within the decade. "We are now within sight of solar energy being the cheapest power source ever created," said one of the study's lead researchers.

Scientists Create Antibodies That Could Stop Epstein-Barr Virus in Its Tracks

Powerful new lab-engineered antibodies block the world's most common cancer-linked virus before it can enter immune cells.

In a potential game-changer for cancer prevention, scientists have engineered potent human-like antibodies capable of stopping the Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) from infecting cells. EBV is carried by more than 90 percent of adults and is linked to several cancers, including certain lymphomas and stomach cancers, as well as multiple sclerosis.

The antibodies were found to be dramatically more effective than previous candidates in laboratory tests, blocking the virus's key attachment proteins and preventing it from entering B-cells — the immune cells it typically hijacks.

Researchers say the findings open a realistic path toward both a preventive vaccine and a treatment for people already infected. A clinical trial is expected to begin within two years, offering hope to millions who live with EBV-related conditions.

France Opens Landmark LGBTQ+ Inclusive Senior Living Community in Lyon

A first-of-its-kind residence in Lyon gives older LGBTQ+ residents a safe, dignified home built around community and belonging.

Lyon has inaugurated one of France's first purpose-built LGBTQ+-inclusive senior living residences, providing a welcoming home for older residents who have long feared discrimination in conventional care settings. The 60-apartment development offers fully accessible housing, on-site social support, and a calendar of community events tailored to residents' interests.

Anchor residents and their families describe the move-in day as "transformative." Several residents in their 70s and 80s shared that it was the first time in decades they felt they could live openly and authentically without fear.

The project is backed by a coalition of local government, LGBTQ+ advocacy groups, and private investors, and organisers hope it will serve as a blueprint for similar communities across France and Europe.

200 Artists Paid to Shine at NCAA Final Four Arts Festival in Indianapolis

The SWISH Arts & Culture Festival turned March Madness week into a city-wide celebration of creativity, paying over 200 local artists.

Indianapolis blended basketball and art in spectacular fashion this spring, when the SWISH Arts & Culture Festival ran alongside the NCAA Men's Final Four from April 3–6. The four-day event filled downtown venues with live music, dance performances, murals, sculpture, and pop-up culinary experiences.

Organisers made it a point to pay every participating artist, performer, chef, and vendor — over 200 local creatives in total — fair market rates, a commitment that drew wide praise and helped keep dollars circulating in the community.

Attendees from across the country discovered Indianapolis's vibrant arts scene for the first time, and city officials reported that hotel bookings and restaurant revenue smashed previous Final Four records. "Sport brings people in; art makes them fall in love with the city," said the festival's creative director.

US Road Deaths Fall to Second Lowest Level Ever Recorded

Traffic fatalities dropped 6.7% last year, marking a major safety milestone and saving thousands of lives.

Road deaths in the United States fell to 36,640 in 2025, a 6.7 percent decline from the previous year and the second-lowest annual total ever recorded, according to new federal data. Safety advocates called the figures encouraging, though they stressed that tens of thousands of deaths remain entirely preventable.

The improvement is credited to a combination of factors: wider adoption of advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS) in new vehicles, expanded infrastructure investment in safer road designs, and targeted enforcement of distracted driving laws.

For the first time, pedestrian fatalities also declined meaningfully after years of alarming increases, dropping 8 percent. Transportation Secretary officials pledged to build on the progress, noting that several cities piloting lower speed limits and protected cycling infrastructure saw the sharpest drops.

Penn State Coaches Pull the Most Delightfully Subtle April Fools' Prank

Footballers almost approved photos of themselves with comically enlarged nostrils and mysteriously shortened arms.

Penn State's coaching staff may have perfected the art of the slow-burn prank this April Fools' Day. They Photoshopped team photos with the most minor, almost imperceptible changes — slightly enlarged nostrils here, a fraction shorter arm there, gently puffed cheeks — and then solemnly asked each player for written approval to post the images on official social media.

The results were glorious. Several players signed off without noticing anything wrong. One linebacker stared at his photo for a full 30 seconds before finally saying, "Wait — is my head... bigger?" Tight ends coach Rob Gillespie was reportedly still laughing 20 minutes after the last player had left the room.

Clips of the players' delayed double-takes spread across social media and racked up millions of views, earning the Penn State program universal goodwill and the unofficial title of best sports prank of 2026.
