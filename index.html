Artemis II Crew Shatters Distance Record on Historic Moon Flyby

Four astronauts traveled 252,756 miles from Earth—farther than any humans since Apollo 17 in 1972.

Four astronauts aboard NASA's Orion spacecraft made history on April 6 when they completed a breathtaking lunar flyby, reaching 252,756 miles from Earth—a new record for the farthest humans have ventured from our planet since Apollo 17 in 1972. The crew of Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Canadian Space Agency astronaut Jeremy Hansen captured more than 7,000 photographs of the Moon's far side during their seven-hour flyby.

Adding to the wonder, the crew witnessed a rare solar eclipse from their extraordinary vantage point, with Commander Wiseman describing the view as "beyond words." All four astronauts splashed down safely. The success of Artemis II paves the way for Artemis III, which aims to land the first woman and first person of color on the lunar surface—a milestone that now feels closer than ever.

Japan's Robots Are Filling Jobs Nobody Wants—and Everyone Needs

Facing a 3.26-million-worker shortfall, Japan is proving that physical AI robots aren't taking jobs—they're saving industries.

Japan's working-age population is shrinking fast—and robots are stepping in to fill the gap. With a labor shortfall of 3.26 million workers, Japanese companies are deploying AI-powered robotic arms across warehouses, construction sites, and elder-care facilities at a pace the rest of the world is watching closely. Tokyo-based Mujin has rolled out robotic systems across 100+ warehouse sites for major retailers, with each station matching the throughput of three to four human workers.

Crucially, the narrative is different here: unlike Western fears of job displacement, these robots are filling roles where there simply are no humans left to hire. Japan's METI ministry aims to capture a 30% share of the global physical AI market by 2040—a sector projected to grow from $307M to $6.76B by 2035.

MIT + Microsoft AI Could Let You Test for 30 Cancers at Home

AI-designed nanoparticle sensors detect cancer-linked proteins in urine—potentially replacing invasive biopsies with a simple at-home kit.

Researchers at MIT and Microsoft Research published a breakthrough in Nature Communications: an AI model designs molecular sensors (peptides) that latch onto enzymes overactive in cancer cells. Nanoparticles coated with these sensors circulate through the body and trigger a signal detectable in urine—no needle, no lab visit required.

Funded by ARPA-H, the team's goal is a single at-home kit that could detect and distinguish between 30 different cancer types in early stages. Early-stage cancer detection is where survival rates improve most dramatically. "We're working toward a future where a urine test catches cancer before symptoms appear," said lead researcher Sangeeta Bhatia.

Google AI Outperforms Radiologists in Landmark Breast Cancer Study

In the largest NHS screening study ever, AI caught more cancers, had fewer false positives, and cut scan-reading time by a third.

A study of 175,000 women published in Nature Cancer found that Google's AI system matched or exceeded radiologists in detecting breast cancer. The AI's cancer detection rate rose to 9.33 per 1,000 women versus 7.54 for human readers—catching more invasive cancers and producing significantly fewer false positives that cause unnecessary anxiety and follow-up procedures.

The efficiency gains are equally striking: AI reduced scan-reading time by 32%, freeing radiologists to focus on complex cases. For first-time scans the AI recalled 39% fewer women while still detecting more cancers. Imperial College London researchers called it "a significant step toward AI-assisted screening becoming standard care."

D-Wave Cracks a Long-Standing Quantum Computing Barrier

The company demonstrated the first-ever scalable on-chip cryogenic control of quantum bits—clearing a key hurdle toward commercial quantum computers.

D-Wave Quantum announced an industry-first breakthrough: scalable, on-chip cryogenic control of gate-model qubits. Until now, quantum computers required massive bundles of wires running into the ultra-cold core to control each qubit—a wiring nightmare that made scaling them nearly impossible. D-Wave's new approach embeds the control electronics on-chip at cryogenic temperatures, dramatically reducing complexity.

"This removes one of the biggest practical obstacles to building a commercially viable quantum computer," said D-Wave's CTO. The company's stock surged more than 200% over the past year as momentum builds, and its planned 2026 dual-rail system will be the first production-ready result of this breakthrough architecture.

New AI Uses 100× Less Power—and Actually Gets Smarter

Tufts researchers cracked a way to slash AI's energy appetite by 99% using neuro-symbolic reasoning, while boosting accuracy from 34% to 95%.

AI already consumes over 10% of US electricity—a figure growing fast. Tufts University researchers may have found the answer: a neuro-symbolic AI system that blends neural networks with human-like logical reasoning, mirroring how people break problems into steps. In Tower of Hanoi tests, it achieved 95% success versus 34% for standard models, on brand-new puzzle configurations it had never seen.

The energy numbers are staggering: training required just 1% of the energy of a conventional system, and runtime power use dropped to 5%. The research will be presented at the International Conference on Robotics and Automation in Vienna this May—and could fundamentally change how we build AI at scale.

NVIDIA's New Robot Brains Understand Plain English—and Actually Work

NVIDIA's GR00T N1.7 model lets humanoid robots understand natural-language instructions and perform complex real-world tasks without custom programming.

NVIDIA released GR00T N1.7, an open foundation model for humanoid robots that enables full-body control through natural language. Rather than programming robots line by line, developers and operators can now simply talk to them. Partners including Franka Robotics and NEURA Robotics are already deploying GR00T-powered workflows to simulate, train, and validate new behaviors in real factories and labs.

At National Robotics Week, NVIDIA also previewed GR00T N2—a next-generation model built on a "world action model" architecture that helps robots succeed at new tasks in unfamiliar environments more than twice as often as leading competitors. The open-source release means thousands of developers worldwide can now build on it immediately.
